# NOTICE — What the Sustainable Use License means, in plain English

ZeroBug Core is source-available under the **Sustainable Use License** (see
[`LICENSE.md`](./LICENSE.md)). That file is the legal text and always wins if anything
here seems to conflict with it. This document is just a friendly explanation so
you don't need a lawyer to understand the one real restriction.

The license is built around a single idea:

> **Use ZeroBug freely — for yourself, your team, or your company. Just don't sell
> ZeroBug-as-a-service to other people as your own product.**

## ✅ You CAN (no permission or payment needed)

- **Self-host it** for yourself, your team, or your entire company — including
  for-profit companies doing real commercial work.
- **Run internal test automation** on your own products with it, at any scale.
- **Modify the code**, fix bugs, add features, and run your modified version
  internally.
- **Use your own API keys** (BYOK) with any model provider.
- **Share your changes** or redistribute the software **free of charge for
  non-commercial purposes**.
- **Evaluate, learn from, and build on the code** for personal or internal use.

Running ZeroBug inside your company to test *your* software is normal internal
use. It's allowed, even if your company is huge and very commercial.

## ❌ You CANNOT (this is the one restriction)

**Offer ZeroBug to third parties as a hosted or managed service.** In other words,
you can't turn this code into a product where *other people* pay you (or sign up)
to use ZeroBug that you operate for them.

Concretely, these are **not** allowed without a separate commercial license:

- Standing up ZeroBug at `yourbrand-testing.com` and letting the public sign up to
  use it (free or paid).
- Selling "ZeroBug hosting" or a managed ZeroBug offering to customers.
- Bundling ZeroBug as the core of a SaaS product you sell, where the value your
  customers pay for is essentially ZeroBug itself.
- Reselling access to a ZeroBug instance you run on someone else's behalf as a
  commercial service.

The distinguishing question is simple: **are you running it for your own
organization's use, or are you operating it as a service for other people to use?**
The first is fine; the second needs a commercial license.

## Grey areas — rule of thumb

- **A consultancy using ZeroBug to deliver testing work to a client** → generally
  fine (you're the operator/user; it's a tool you use internally to do your job),
  as long as you're not selling access to a ZeroBug instance as the product.
- **An agency that hosts a ZeroBug login for each client to self-serve** → that's
  operating it as a service for others — not allowed without a commercial license.
- If you're unsure whether your use crosses the line, ask us first (below). We're
  happy to clarify, and the honest internal-use cases are almost always fine.
